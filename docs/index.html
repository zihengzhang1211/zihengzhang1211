About Me

I’m a UCLA graduate student pursuing my MS in Biostatistics at the Fielding School of Public Health, with a research focus on Bayesian methods and their applications in neuroimaging. My work combines dynamic functional connectivity analysis from resting-state fMRI data with advanced statistical tools, including Gaussian graphical models and causal mediation analysis, to study the brain mechanisms underlying neurodegenerative diseases such as frontotemporal dementia (FTD).

I’m passionate about mathematical and statistical methods for Bayesian modeling and brain connectivity analysis to push the boundaries of computational neuroscience and brain-computer interfaces, and to contribute to the future of diagnosis and treatment in neurodegenerative diseases.

Research Interests

  1. Bayesian Methods for Brain Connectivity Analysis
    Development of Bayesian graphical models and causal mediation frameworks to investigate dynamic functional connectivity in neurodegenerative diseases, with a focus on frontotemporal dementia (FTD) and its genetic underpinnings.

  2. Bayesian Methods in Phase I Clinical Trials
    Application of Bayesian methods to phase I clinical trial design under small sample sizes and uncertainty, enhancing the efficiency and reliability of safety evaluations in drug development.

  3. Causal Inference in Neuroimaging and Neurogenetics
    Modeling the mediating role of brain functional connectivity in linking genetic mutation to cognitive and behavioral outcomes using model-based causal inference methods.

  4. False Discovery Rate Control in High-Dimensional Settings
    Integration of Bayesian modeling approaches into FDR control procedures for genomic data, aiming to improve interpretability, flexibility, and robustness over traditional methods.
